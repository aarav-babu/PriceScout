FROM CAPSTONE TO TODAY

A research idea.
An independently maintained product.

I’m Aarav Babu, the sole maintainer of PriceScout today. The idea grew out of a capstone project exploring web data and machine learning for resale pricing. I’m continuing that work as a practical tool for researching used products.

The question behind the research still matters: how useful is a price estimate if its data no longer reflects the market? The original study explored collecting relevant listings at query time, preparing that data, and comparing regression models on small used-car datasets.

Today’s public app compares observed car asking prices in India and saves phones and laptops in a private collection. Machine-learning valuations and phone/laptop pricing are not part of this release.

01

Markets move. Data ages.

Demand, new models, and available inventory change. A fixed dataset can describe yesterday’s market while you’re making a decision today.

02

Start with current evidence

PriceScout reads current public listings when a search needs fresh data. Results include source links and a checked time; matching searches share a snapshot for up to five minutes.

03

Keep the limits visible

A listing is a seller’s asking price, not a completed sale. The app shows a first-page sample and reports when prices cannot be retrieved.
